## Direction

The user supplied three reference images: a loose pencil figure, a layered game-journal spread and a sketch of a personal music player. Their intent is imperfect human marks and tangible mounted paper, not a uniform vintage tint. Cover: scuffed charcoal. Handwriting: locally hosted Kalam, regular for titles and annotations, with readable serif/monospace for research data and longer methods.

## Prompt set

Shared initial prompt: Use case: illustration-story. Asset type: graphite drawing for a tactile research notebook website, not a UI mockup. Medium: believable hand-drawn 2B graphite pencil, slightly hesitant overlapping contours, fine cross-hatching, erased construction lines and pressure variation, like a careful personal notebook study of a real object. Warm dark graphite only, no green, no paint, no cartoon, no perfect vector lines. Composition: landscape, isolated objects centered, generous transparent margins. No text, numbers, lettering, symbols, arrows, border, tape, paper background or cast shadow; the website supplies the paper and labels. Transparent background. This is a conceptual illustration, not a scientific data diagram.

Memory subject: A small open wooden desk drawer seen at a slight overhead angle, its interior empty except the faint erased outline of a key; beside it a realistic folded jacket with a clearly recognizable stitched patch pocket, with a single antique brass key half tucked into that pocket. The same key used to be in the drawer and has moved to the pocket. The drawer and pocket must read as two distinct physical locations. Make the actual key, wood joints and fabric seams charmingly observed, not decorative clutter.

Queue subject: Three overlapping paper admission tickets in a neat staggered waiting line, seen overhead, with perforated edges and a small old-fashioned mechanical ticket dispenser at one end. One middle ticket has its corner folded, distinguishing it. This drawing conveys that earlier tickets are served before later tickets. Study the curl of the paper, torn perforation fibres and dispenser mechanism. Tickets completely blank, no labels, no barcodes, no numbers. A coherent pencil still-life, not a flow chart.

Impact subject: A small mechanical weather barometer with round dial and a folded umbrella lying beside it, seen at a slight overhead angle. The dial can have unlabeled tick marks and a needle, absolutely no numbers or words. This conceptual still-life contrasts forecasting the weather with deciding whether to take an umbrella: a prediction is separate from a useful action. Detailed observed mechanism, worn handle and fabric folds, compact readable silhouette, no extra props.

All three first drafts were too heavily shaded. The final edit prompt, with the first draft as image 1 and the user's small drawings as images 2 and 3:

Style-transfer edit. Image 1 is the edit target; images 2 and 3 are ONLY pencil drawing style references. Redraw the same objects and arrangement from image 1 as a sparse, casual human graphite contour sketch matching the scratchy, imperfect notebook drawings in images 2 and 3. Crucial: mostly empty negative space INSIDE the objects, thin broken graphite outlines, a few quick cross-hatches, doubled exploratory lines and faint construction lines. Not a photoreal rendering, no solid filled surfaces, no polished illustration, no dense shading, no color or brass color, no paper background or cast shadow or grey halo. Do NOT copy any object or writing from the style references. Preserve the subjects and clear concept from image 1. No lettering or annotations. Genuine transparent background and transparent interiors between the pencil strokes. Landscape composition. Think of an ordinary person making an observant sketch with a blunt pencil in two minutes.
